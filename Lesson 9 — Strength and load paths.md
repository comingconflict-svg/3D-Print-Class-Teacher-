# Lesson 9 — Strength and load paths

**Time:** 45 minutes main lesson  
**Goal:** Students make a part meant to hold, hang, or clip, and they can point to the place that would break first.

## Learning targets
1. Open Lesson 9 from the student repo.
2. Find the thin corner or short foot that will fail.
3. Add material where the load goes: thicker wall, fillet, wider base.
4. Use 3 walls on load-bearing parts. Infill 15–30%.
5. Do not use 100% infill as the first idea.

## Design challenge — pick one
- Key hook that must hold keys or a small bag
- Cable clip that must flex without snapping
- Phone stand that must not face-plant
- Repair of an earlier object that already broke

Max size: 90 mm. No decorative spikes thinner than 2 mm.

## Strength rules
1. Load travels through material. A 1 mm neck is a break line.
2. Outside walls usually beat 100% infill on small parts.
3. A rounded inside corner is stronger than a sharp inside corner.
4. A wider foot beats a taller skinny stem.

## Teacher prep
- [ ] Broken class parts on the table
- [ ] One strong hook and one weak hook
- [ ] Calipers
- [ ] Lesson 9 student file is in the student repo

## Agenda

### 0:00–0:05 — GitHub
Open Lesson 9.

### 0:05–0:15 — Break line hunt
Pass around weak and strong examples. Students point to the failure spot first.

### 0:15–0:22 — What they may change
Allowed: 3 walls, 30% infill, fillet, thicker neck, wider foot.  
Not allowed: mystery profiles or tiny layers today.

### 0:22–0:42 — Build or repair
Checkpoint: student can point to the load path on their own model.

### 0:42–0:45 — Queue
YYYY-MM-DD_Firstname_Strong_v1.stl plus the one strength change on the sheet.

## Success check
Student names the old weak spot and the one strength change.
